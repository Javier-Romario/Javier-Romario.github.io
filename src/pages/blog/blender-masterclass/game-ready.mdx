## The rule

<Formula math="Tris cost GPU, quads keep you sane" note="Model in quads (they subdivide and deform cleanly), let the engine convert to triangles at load." />

## Triangle budgets, by the numbers

```text
prop / pickup           < 1,000  tris
character               < 30,000 tris
hero character          50k–100k (head + body)
whole scene             keep it as low as looks right
```

Every time you subdivide, you **×4** the triangles.

<div class="poke">Bump SUBDIVISIONS from 0 to 4. The triangle count jumps 20 → 320 → 1,280 →
5,120 → 20,480. Same silhouette, wildly different GPU cost. That's the whole budget game.</div>

## Retopology: rebuild it clean

After sculpting or boolean chaos, you **retopologize** — draw a fresh, low, all-quad mesh over
the high-res shape. In Blender: enable the **Bsurfaces** or use the built-in **Poly Build** tool
(`Ctrl` + click to place quads) with snapping turned on.

```text
Shift+Tab         snap toggle → set to "Face" to stick new quads to the high-res surface
```

## Normals: the free detail

A **normal** is the direction a face points. Smooth shading (`Shade Auto Smooth`) fakes roundness
on a low mesh — the renderer blends normals instead of adding geometry. Same triangles, looks
rounder. Free detail is the entire art of the low-poly model.
