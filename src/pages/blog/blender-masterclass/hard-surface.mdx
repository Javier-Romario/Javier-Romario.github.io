## Bevel = the light-catcher

No real object has a mathematically sharp edge. A **bevel** (or chamfer) rounds or flats that
edge so light glints off it. In Blender: add a **Bevel** modifier, or select an edge loop and
press `Ctrl+B`.

```text
Ctrl+B          bevel selected edges (drag, then scroll for segments)
Ctrl+Shift+B    vertex bevel
```

<div class="poke">Drag the BEVEL RADIUS slider. 0 = razor edge, 0.5 = soft chamfer. Flip WIREFRAME
to see how a bevel is just extra geometry along the edge.</div>

## Boolean = union & subtract

A **boolean** is the CAD-style operation: union two parts, or cut a hole with one. In Blender 4.x
it's just a modifier — keep it **non-destructive** and you can slide the cutting shape around.

```text
Add a Boolean modifier  →  choose Union / Difference / Intersect
Pick the Object to cut with  →  move it live, watch the cut follow
```

Booleans leave messy topology behind. Fix it with **Ctrl+J** joins, **M** merge-by-distance, and
a pass of **limited dissolve** (`X` → Limited Dissolve) to kill junk vertices.

## Clean topology = the rule of quads

A **quad** is a 4-sided face. Quads subdivide and deform predictably. Triangles are fine at the
end, but model in quads, let Blender convert to triangles at export.

<div class="whycare">A printer only cares that the mesh is watertight. A GPU cares how MANY
triangles you feed it. Quads let you control both.</div>
